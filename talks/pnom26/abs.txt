Spatial and Temporal Visual Masking Reflect Distinct Dimensions of Individual Differences in Visual Masking

Visual masking provides a framework for studying how humans resolve information across space and time. In what we call spatial masking, the target and mask are presented simultaneously requiring observers to separate signal from overlapping noise.  The task measures the observer's spatial abilities. In temporal masking, the mask follows the target after a brief delay requiring rapid processing of the target signal before it is interrupted by subsequent visual noise.  The task measures the observer's temporal abilities. By comparing spatial and temporal masking using identical stimuli and masks, the present study examines whether these two masking paradigms reflect shared or dissociable perceptual abilities. Surprisingly, preliminary findings suggest that spatial masking performance shows stronger associations with working memory and intelligence than temporal masking performance. Besides, substantial stimulus-specific effects appear, suggesting that performance is partly driven by stimulus-specific abilities shared across spatial and temporal masking tasks.
